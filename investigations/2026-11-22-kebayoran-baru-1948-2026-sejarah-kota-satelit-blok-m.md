## Summary

Menelusuri perencanaan Kebayoran Baru sejak 1948 sebagai kota satelit di selatan Jakarta, latar kekurangan perumahan pascaperang, peran H. Mohammad Soesilo dan pengaruh gagasan perencanaan Thomas Karsten, serta pembangunan oleh Centrale Stichting Wederopbouw (CSW). Artikel mencatat luas rencana sekitar 730 hektare, penyelesaian formal pada 1954 dengan target perumahan yang belum sepenuhnya tercapai, dan persoalan pembebasan tanah serta kampung-kampung yang terdampak. Bagian berikutnya membahas blok-blok kota, perubahan fungsi hunian dan perdagangan, serta perjalanan Blok M dari pusat komersial dan ruang pergaulan anak muda menuju fase penurunan dan revitalisasi. Pertanyaan tentang komunitas Tionghoa dan perubahan sosial ditempatkan sebagai agenda riset, bukan klaim komposisi etnis. Tidak menyimpulkan bahwa perubahan fungsi lahan, harga properti, atau lokasi usaha membuktikan penguasaan kawasan oleh kelompok tertentu.

## Evidence and scope notes

- The planning chronology, the 1948 conception, the role attributed to H. Mohammad Soesilo, the influence of Thomas Karsten's planning ideas, the approximate 730-hectare plan, and the 1954 completion milestone are discussed with reference to Erwin Fahmi's peer-reviewed study.
- The article treats land acquisition, displacement, and the relationship between the planned town and pre-existing kampungs as historical questions requiring careful attention to the available scholarship and primary records.
- Blok M's changing social and urban role is discussed with reference to the 2025 University of Indonesia study on production of space and urban memory.
- Urban morphology and the shift between residential and commercial functions are treated as planning and land-use questions, not as evidence of ethnic identity or ownership.

## Open questions

- Which primary land-acquisition maps, administrative records, and historical cadastral documents identify the kampungs and parcels affected by the original development?
- Which residents were relocated, compensated, or remained within and around the planned town, and what records support each account?
- How did land-use changes across the A–S blocks occur over time, and what planning decisions or market forces can be documented?
- What sources can establish the histories of Tionghoa residents, organizations, schools, religious institutions, and businesses without inferring ethnicity from addresses or commercial presence?

## Method note

Keep the planned-city history, documented land acquisition, later land-use changes, and social-history questions analytically separate. Do not treat neighborhood reputation, property prices, place names, business clusters, or visual impressions as proof of ethnic composition, ownership, or political networks. Distinguish documented facts, reported claims, interpretation, and unknowns.
