## Scope and summary

The published BLOON Investigation article revisits the 1981 Setiabudi 13 case: the discovery of human remains in two cardboard boxes on a sidewalk in the Sudirman–Setiabudi area of South Jakarta. The article treats the case as one victim, not thirteen victims, and distinguishes the figure “13” from other press accounts referring to 181 pieces or fragments.

The central questions are the victim's identity, the perpetrator or perpetrators, the motive, the timeline between death and disposal, the forensic evidence, and the reasons the case has remained publicly unresolved.

## Main points recorded in the publication

1. **Discovery:** Two security guards of PT Garuda Mataram Motor, identified in the reporting as Suriadi Jaya and Ade Sumarna, reportedly noticed a foul smell from two boxes on 23 November 1981 and alerted police.
2. **Victim count and fragment counts:** The article states that the case concerns one human victim. It records different figures in media descriptions—13 body parts in one account and 181 pieces in others—and cautions that these may refer to different counting conventions (larger anatomical parts versus smaller tissue fragments). The exact count requires the original forensic inventory.
3. **Forensic examination:** The article discusses reporting about forensic specialist dr. Abdul Mun'im Idris. It records descriptions of the condition of the remains, a reported suspicion that a hacksaw may have been used based on marks on bone, and the expert's reported view that more than one person may have been involved. These are reported forensic assessments, not proof that a specific tool was recovered or that the number of perpetrators is known.
4. **Victim profile:** Press accounts of the forensic description estimate a male victim aged approximately 18–21 and around 165 cm tall, with other physical characteristics. The article notes that the identity remains publicly unresolved in the reporting reviewed.
5. **Packaging and newspaper clues:** Reported items include plastic bags bearing a shop name associated with Pasar Baru, another supermarket bag, and three copies of an evening newspaper dated 19 August 1981. These are investigative leads, not proof of the involvement of any shop, seller, or newspaper distributor.
6. **Timeline:** The discovery date is 23 November 1981. A press account reportedly placed the likely time of death around 21 November. The article distinguishes estimated death, dismemberment, transport/disposal, and discovery as separate events.
7. **Motive:** Revenge and deliberate placement in a public location are mentioned as possibilities in secondary reporting. The article does not treat either as an established motive.
8. **Unresolved investigative questions:** The article asks whether original fingerprint checks, forensic photographs, autopsy reports, evidence inventories, missing-person records, scene reconstruction, and witness statements remain accessible.
9. **“First mutilation case” claim:** The article questions the common media description of Setiabudi 13 as Indonesia's first mutilation case. It cites reporting that quotes Enoch Markum referring to multiple mutilation cases recorded since 1967, and therefore avoids presenting “first” as an established historical fact.

## Open research questions

- What does the original autopsy report say about the number and classification of body parts and fragments?
- Is there an original forensic inventory that explains the difference between “13” and “181”?
- Are the forensic photographs, fingerprint records, and laboratory notes preserved?
- What is the exact wording and context of dr. Abdul Mun'im Idris's original statements?
- Were the plastic bags and newspaper traced to specific retail outlets or distribution points?
- What missing-person reports were received, and are the underlying records accessible?
- What primary police records document the investigative steps, witnesses, and case status?
- Is there reliable archival evidence for describing the case as Indonesia's first mutilation case?
