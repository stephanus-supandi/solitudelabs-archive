## Editorial scope

This article introduces Robert Zünd (1827–1909), the Swiss landscape painter from Lucerne, through his close observation of forests, vegetation, natural light, and rural landscapes. It focuses on the *Eichwald* / *Eichenwald* oak-forest paintings and distinguishes naturalistic representation from literal photographic reproduction.

## Article summary

The article outlines Zünd's life and artistic formation in Switzerland and Geneva, including his study in the artistic environment of François Diday and Alexandre Calame. It discusses his attention to tree trunks, foliage, undergrowth, spatial depth, and changing light; the 1859 *Eichwald* in Kunstmuseum Luzern; and the larger *Eichenwald* version dated around 1881–1882 in Kunsthaus Zürich.

It explains that Zünd's landscapes combine close observation with artistic selection and idealization. The article also mentions his studies made from nature, the atmospheric *Storm-Ridden Landscape* (1854), and interests beyond landscape painting, including religious subjects and painting on glass. Its central interpretation is that careful observation—not merely the accumulation of detail—gives the paintings their visual structure and atmosphere.
