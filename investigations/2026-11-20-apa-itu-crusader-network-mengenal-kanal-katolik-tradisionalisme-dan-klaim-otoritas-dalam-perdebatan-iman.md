# Apa Itu CRUSADER Network? Mengenal Kanal Katolik, Tradisionalisme, dan Klaim Otoritas dalam Perdebatan Iman

## Editorial scope

An initial public-source examination of CRUSADER Network: its publicly associated figure, stated orientation, recurring subject areas, claims about Catholic teaching, relationship to ecclesial authority, and publicly advertised support channels. The article is an independent media-literacy and theological review, not an official Church statement.

## Article summary

The article identifies Agustinus Daniel as the figure associated with CRUSADER Network on the basis of a publisher biography for his book *Bencana Konsili Vatikan II*. That biography describes him as a lay Catholic based in Bandung, managing the CRUSADER Network YouTube channel and leading the virtual lay community LEGIO CHRISTI REGIS. These details are attributed to the publisher's public biography and are not presented as an independent audit of his personal or institutional status.

The article examines the channel's own 17 June 2022 explanation of its name and orientation. That statement describes a militant “soldier of Christ” framing and identifies apologetics toward other religions, particularly Islam, and criticism of developments in the Catholic Church after Vatican II as major activities. The article then reviews examples of public content on Islam, Vatican II, Protestant and Orthodox communities, and Catholic devotions.

A central distinction is maintained between a lay person's right to discuss theology and the authority of the Church's Magisterium to teach authentically. The article uses *Lumen Gentium* 25, *Dei Verbum* 10, and Catechism of the Catholic Church §§2032–2036 to explain the distinction. It also uses *Nostra Aetate* 3 as a primary reference for the Church's teaching concerning Muslims, while distinguishing respect and dialogue from religious syncretism or the claim that all religious doctrines are identical.

The article records publicly advertised support channels associated with CRUSADER Network, including QRIS, Patreon, SubscribeStar, Buy Me a Coffee, Saweria, bank transfer, digital books, merchandise, and religious icons, as described in the article's reviewed material. The existence of donation or sales channels is not treated as evidence of wrongdoing. The article argues only that transparency about the activity being supported and the use of funds is relevant when a channel exercises influence through religious content.

## Method note

Distinguish a channel's self-description, a publisher's author biography, observed public content, theological interpretation, and official ecclesial authority. Do not infer official diocesan status from Catholic identity, devotional practices, or personal contacts. Do not infer misconduct from the existence of donations or merchandise. Claims about doctrine must be assessed against primary Church documents in context; criticism of a council or liturgical reform must not be conflated automatically with a formal act of schism or heresy.
