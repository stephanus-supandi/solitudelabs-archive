## Editorial scope

This archive record covers Luigi Loir (1845–1916), the French painter, illustrator, lithographer, and decorative artist known for depictions of Parisian urban life. It distinguishes his French artistic identity from his birthplace in Göritz/Gorizia, then part of the Austrian Empire. The article connects his paintings to the changing urban environment of Paris while stressing that paintings are artistic interpretations, not precise photographic or cadastral records.

## Article summary

The article follows Loir's life from his birth on 22 December 1845 in Göritz/Gorizia, then within the Austrian Empire, through his family's move to Parma, his art education, and his move to Paris in 1863. It discusses his 1865 Salon debut and his later reputation for paintings of Parisian streets, boulevards, public spaces, weather, changing light, and everyday urban activity.

Selected works discussed include *Le boulevard du Palais, vu de la place Saint-Michel* (1888), *Porte Maillot, effet de neige, la nuit* (c. 1900), *La rue des Nations, à l'Exposition Universelle de 1900* (1900), works associated with the Franco-Russian celebrations of 1896, *La neige, place de la République*, and *Les travaux du Métropolitain, rue de Rivoli ; effet de crépuscule*.

The article places Loir's work in the context of nineteenth-century Parisian urban transformation, including Haussmann-era redevelopment, public space, transport, and the 1900 Exposition Universelle. It argues that his paintings can serve as historical visual sources when read alongside maps, photographs, museum catalogues, and urban records, while avoiding the claim that a painting alone establishes exact building identity, dimensions, or location.

The closing section draws an editorial comparison between historical city painting, modern Street View imagery, and the BLOON Economic Observatory's approach to dated, source-traceable urban observation. This is a methodological analogy, not a claim that the media have equivalent evidentiary properties.

## Method note

Separate birthplace, nationality, artistic identity, and later reputation. Distinguish a museum catalogue's title/date/location metadata from visual interpretation. A painting can preserve evidence of urban appearance and atmosphere, but it is not a photograph, property record, business register, or precise survey. Do not infer exact addresses or surviving building identity from a suggestive resemblance alone. The comparison with Google Street View and BLOON is an editorial analogy; source type, date precision, and evidentiary scope remain different.
