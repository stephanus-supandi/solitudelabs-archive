## Scope and summary

The article examines Gita Wirjawan's documented education and institutional affiliations, the appearance of Harvard, Stanford, and MIT in his public-facing education discussions, and the broader relationship between elite academic credentials, competence, intellectual quality, and personal branding.

It distinguishes:
- Gita Wirjawan's Master of Public Administration from Harvard Kennedy School, which is part of Harvard University;
- his Stanford visiting-scholar affiliation, which is not a Stanford degree;
- an *Endgame* episode collaboration with MIT Sloan, which is not evidence of an MIT degree or alumni status.

The article treats personal branding as an analytical interpretation of public positioning, not as a proven private motive. It does not claim that Gita is obsessed with particular universities, nor does it infer hidden motives from repeated references to elite institutions.

Its broader argument is that institutional credentials can provide useful context and networks but do not independently establish the truth of every claim. Credentials, demonstrated competence, and the quality of reasoning should be evaluated separately. Arguments should be tested through evidence, methods, applicability, and outcomes rather than accepted or rejected solely because of the speaker's university affiliation.

## Evidence and method notes

- Harvard Kennedy School is part of Harvard University; the article identifies Gita's MPA as a degree, not merely a visiting affiliation.
- Stanford visiting scholar and Harvard visiting fellow are institutional affiliations and must not be conflated with degrees from those institutions.
- The MIT reference concerns a podcast collaboration with MIT Sloan; it is not represented as an MIT qualification.
- The article's discussion of personal branding is explicitly interpretive. Public profile and institutional affiliation do not establish a person's private motivation.
- A casual use of the word “dakwah” is not treated as evidence of religious preaching; the specific recording and context would need to be examined.
- The article does not assess the truth of every statement made in *Endgame* episodes. That would require episode-specific transcript and claim-level review.
