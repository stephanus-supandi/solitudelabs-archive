## Editorial scope

This article distinguishes the fictional character Dasiyah/Jeng Yah, the real history of the kretek industry and railway infrastructure, and the real filming location at Stasiun Tuntang. A real location used in a fictional scene does not make the scene itself a historical event.

## Article

### From a kretek story to a real railway station

*Gadis Kretek* is an Indonesian Netflix drama series released on 2 November 2023 and adapted from Ratih Kumala's novel of the same title, published in 2012. The series was directed by Kamila Andini and Ifa Isfansyah and produced by BASE Entertainment.

The story moves between Dasiyah's life around the 1960s and a later search by the next generation for secrets from the past. The principal characters include Dasiyah/Jeng Yah (Dian Sastrowardoyo), Soeraja/Mas Raja (Ario Bayu), Arum (Putri Marino), and Lebas (Arya Saloka).

### Who is the real Gadis Kretek?

Within the story, the answer is Dasiyah, known as Jeng Yah. She is the daughter of a kretek entrepreneur and wants to develop her own kretek sauce blend. The industry is portrayed as a male-dominated world, and Dasiyah's ambition confronts social expectations and restrictions placed on women.

Dasiyah is a fictional character created by Ratih Kumala, not a historical individual whose exact life story has been independently established.

In a January 2024 interview with kumparanWOMAN, Ratih explained that the novel's kretek-industry setting drew inspiration from her maternal extended family. Her grandfather had been a kretek entrepreneur in a small Central Javanese town. Ratih recalled family stories about a house used to store dried tobacco and a place where workers rolled cigarettes. For the novel, she also conducted research and visited several Central Javanese cities, including Kudus, Magelang, and Semarang.

Ratih described Dasiyah as drawing on more than one woman she knew. Names such as Yah, Jeng Yah, Dasiyah, and Rukayah were inspired by names in her extended family. These inspirations and research provide a real-world background, but the characters and plot remain fiction.

### Why the title *Gadis Kretek*?

Kretek is not merely a prop in the drama. It is part of Indonesia's social and economic history, particularly in Central Java. In a November 2023 interview reported by Kompas.com, Ratih explained that *Gadis Kretek* is also the name of a cigarette brand used within the story. The title therefore connects the fictional woman with the kretek business that forms the story's setting.

### Stasiun Tuntang: a real place in the series

Stasiun Tuntang is located in Tuntang District, Semarang Regency, Central Java. It was not built as a Netflix set. According to detikJateng's 27 November 2023 historical report, the station was built in 1871 and began operating on 21 May 1873 as part of the railway network in the Dutch East Indies.

The station appears in *Gadis Kretek* in the scene depicting the final meeting between Mas Raja and Jeng Yah. The scene is fictional; the station building and its railway history are real.

Historically, Tuntang played a role in moving plantation commodities toward Ambarawa. The commodities reported include rubber, sugar, coffee, and chocolate. The station also served as a transfer point for a Nederlandsch-Indische Spoorweg Maatschappij (NIS) bus connection between Tuntang and Salatiga. In 1921, the bus service was taken over by the private Eerste Salatigasche Transport Onderneming (ESTO).

This is a reminder that intercity transport in the colonial period could combine railway and road services.

### Why did the station stop operating?

Stasiun Tuntang was deactivated on 1 June 1970. The Ambarawa–Tuntang line later returned for tourist journeys in 2002 after renovation. Further development and repair work, including work from 2009, supported the vintage railway tourism operation.

Today, Tuntang forms part of the Ambarawa railway heritage tourism area.

### The Ambarawa–Tuntang tourist railway

A visit can begin at Museum Kereta Api Ambarawa, formerly Stasiun Willem I. KAI Wisata describes a tourist route from the museum to Stasiun Tuntang and back to Ambarawa. The round trip is described as taking approximately 60–90 minutes.

The journey offers views of rural landscapes, rice fields, the Rawa Pening area, and surrounding mountains. It is not simply a ride to a filming location; it is also a way to experience part of Indonesia's railway heritage.

Two tourist routes should not be confused:

**Ambarawa–Tuntang**
- Starts at Museum Kereta Api Ambarawa.
- Travels to Stasiun Tuntang and returns to Ambarawa.
- Passes rural scenery, rice fields, and the Rawa Pening area.

**Ambarawa–Jambu–Bedono**
- Travels through hilly terrain.
- Includes a rack railway section.
- Uses special railway engineering to help trains negotiate steeper gradients.
- Offers a different experience from the Tuntang route.

KAI Wisata lists both relations as Museum Ambarawa tourist routes. Operating days, locomotive types, schedules, fares, and availability can change; visitors should confirm current details with the operator before travelling.

### Can Mount Merbabu be seen from the train?

Yes. Mount Merbabu can be visible from the Ambarawa–Tuntang area when weather and visibility cooperate. ANTARA has described the tourist journey as offering rice-field and hill scenery with Mount Merbabu in the background. The Rawa Pening landscape is also part of the view.

Other mountains associated with the wider Ambarawa landscape include Mount Telomoyo and Mount Ungaran. Whether a particular mountain is visible depends on the train's position, viewing direction, clouds, haze, and fog. A clear view is never guaranteed.

### Rawa Pening: more than a backdrop

Rawa Pening is a natural lake in Semarang Regency, surrounded by hills and mountains. The landscape contributes to the character of the Ambarawa–Tuntang journey. From the train, visitors may see water, rice fields, and aspects of local life around the lake.

The lake is not merely a cinematic background. It is also an ecological and economic landscape for surrounding communities. The railway journey therefore brings together transport history, rural life, and Central Java's geography.

### Fiction and history must remain distinct

Does *Gadis Kretek* accurately depict the history of Indonesia's kretek industry? The answer requires care.

Three layers should be kept separate:

1. **Historical context:** Indonesia's kretek industry, tobacco trade, workers, businesses, and transport networks are real subjects of economic and social history.
2. **Real-life inspiration:** Ratih Kumala has described family stories and research that informed the novel's setting and characters.
3. **Fiction:** Dasiyah, Soeraja, their romance, family conflicts, and the specific sequence of events are elements of the fictional narrative.

The same distinction applies to Stasiun Tuntang. The station is real, its operational history can be researched, and it was used as a filming location. The events experienced there by the characters remain part of the drama.

Separating these layers lets audiences enjoy fiction without turning it into unsupported history.

### Planning a visit

Museum Kereta Api Ambarawa is a practical starting point for visitors interested in the station and its railway heritage. Depending on current operations, visitors may be able to:

1. Explore locomotives and historical railway objects at the museum.
2. Take the tourist train toward Stasiun Tuntang.
3. Enjoy views of Rawa Pening.
4. Observe rice fields and the surrounding mountain landscape.
5. See the station building used as a filming location for *Gadis Kretek*.

Official operator information: https://kaiwisata.id/Museum-Ambarawa

Schedules, fares, and availability should be checked directly with KAI Wisata before departure.

## Conclusion: a small station and a long story

*Gadis Kretek* brings two worlds together. On one side is Dasiyah, a fictional woman with ambitions in a kretek industry portrayed as male-dominated. On the other is Stasiun Tuntang, a real building that has passed through the colonial period, changes in transport, deactivation, and its return as part of railway heritage tourism.

From Tuntang, the tourist railway journey connects visitors with Ambarawa and the Rawa Pening landscape. Mount Merbabu may appear in the distance when the weather is clear.

A television series can become an entry point for learning about history, geography, transport, and culture. *Gadis Kretek* is fiction, but the station is real, the railway is real, and the history behind them can still be investigated.
