## Summary

The article investigates the founding of Gereja Santo Bartolomeus / Paroki Taman Galaxi in Bekasi Selatan, the origin and administrative allocation of its land, the roles of parishioners, the developer/estate, local government, and the Archdiocese of Jakarta (KAJ), the reported fundraising and construction of the main church, and the still-unresolved history of the Gedung Karya Pastoral (GKP).

According to testimony reproduced in the parish's 30th-anniversary history book, the initial idea for a new church in Bekasi Selatan emerged in early 1993 and was associated with Philipus Winarso, then Deputy Chair II of the Santo Arnoldus Parish Daily Council. He convened more than 30 lay representatives to discuss the need for a new church. The plan was brought to the Santo Arnoldus parish council and received approval; the broader pastoral expansion also considered Bekasi Utara, Cikarang, and Rawalumbu.

The article distinguishes KAJ's ecclesiastical approval and guidance from direct responsibility for all project costs. The historical account describes searches for alternative land, an unsuccessful approach involving land in Kemang Pratama, and a later approach to Pak Halim, identified in the book as the owner of the Taman Galaksi estate. The account says approximately 4,000 m² of FASOS was offered, after which the local government allocated the site among three churches; the Catholic share is reported as 2,000 m². A principle-use permit for FASOS is reported as issued on 14 December 1994. These are claims in the parish's retrospective narrative, not independently verified land-title findings.

The article describes multiple fundraising channels for the main church: parishioner contributions collected through local communities, personal fundraising, production and sale of religious music cassettes, assistance from other KAJ parishes, and a fundraising dinner at Hotel Sahid. The retrospective account reports approximately Rp1.8 billion in construction costs for the main church. The article explicitly does not attribute that amount to the GKP.

Construction arrangements described in the historical account include parish-led planning and supervision, a contractor selected through tender for construction, and volunteer/parish-led finishing work, with the arrangements reportedly approved by KAJ. The main church began being used for Mass on 18 August 1996 and was blessed on 5 April 1997. The parish's formation date is reported as 20 September 1995.

A central unresolved question is the GKP's own development history. The article does not claim to know its initiator, decision date, construction committee, budget, funding sources, contractor, designer, or whether it was built in stages. It calls for parish council minutes, financial statements, construction committee reports, blessing records, and project archives before those details can be established.

The article also records the historical account's description of early engagement with surrounding residents, including requests for local employment opportunities, the hiring of Mustofa, youth involvement in parking security, and a reported first communal iftar at the GKP. These examples are presented as documented recollections in the parish publication, not proof of any broader hidden organizational relationship.

## Key distinctions and caveats

- The reported Rp1.8 billion refers to the main church building in the retrospective account; it is **not** established as the GKP budget.
- The reported 2,000 m² Catholic allocation and 14 December 1994 FASOS permit are drawn from the parish history narrative. Verify with the original permit, land-use agreement, cadastral records, and government archive before treating them as definitive legal title or parcel measurements.
- KAJ approval/guidance, parish fundraising, land provision, estate recommendation, and local-government allocation are distinct roles; they should not be collapsed into a single source of ownership or financing.
- The GKP's initiator, budget, funding, contractor, designer, and construction chronology remain unresolved.
- The historical account is a retrospective parish publication. Its testimony is a useful primary-source lead, but financial and land claims require corroboration with contemporaneous records.
- No allegation of improper financing, control, or wrongdoing is made against the parish, KAJ, estate, government, or named individuals.
- The article is an editorial investigation, not an audit of parish finances or a legal opinion on land status.

## Open questions

1. What are the original land-use agreement, FASOS allocation decision, principle permit, and cadastral/land-registration records for the church parcel?
2. What contemporaneous parish and KAJ minutes document the 1993–1997 decisions and division of responsibilities?
3. What led to the construction of the GKP, and when was the project formally approved?
4. Who chaired the GKP construction committee, and who designed and built it?
5. What were the GKP's approved budget, actual expenditure, funding sources, and financial reporting?
6. Was the GKP constructed in one phase or several phases, and when was it blessed or first used?
7. What original records support the reported Rp1.8 billion main-church construction cost?
8. What parish records document early community engagement, local employment, parking arrangements, and the first communal iftar?

## Method note

Both publication pages were retrieved and reviewed on 2026-11-23. The archive record summarizes the published investigation; it does not reproduce the article verbatim. The article relies substantially on a retrospective parish history book and reported testimony. The underlying land instruments, contemporaneous minutes, project accounts, contractor documents, and GKP-specific records were not independently inspected for this archive update.
