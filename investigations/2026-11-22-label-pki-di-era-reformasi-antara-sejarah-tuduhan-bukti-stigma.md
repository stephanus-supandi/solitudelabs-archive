## Archive summary

Examines the use of the label “PKI” in post-Reformasi Indonesia and distinguishes documented organizational history from allegations about individual people. The article argues that family relationship, friendship, neighborhood, reading history, or conducting academic research do not by themselves establish membership, support, or criminal involvement. It separates documented facts, testimony or reported information, and allegations; stresses source criticism and corroboration; and discusses the post-1998 context, the continued status of TAP MPRS XXV/MPRS/1966 under TAP MPR I/MPR/2003, and the scientific-study exception in Article 188(6) of UU No. 1/2023. It also distinguishes public debate from a legal determination of guilt and notes the relevance of KUHP Articles 433–434 when discussing accusations against living people. The conclusion calls for evidence-based historical research and rejects guilt by family association or social proximity.
