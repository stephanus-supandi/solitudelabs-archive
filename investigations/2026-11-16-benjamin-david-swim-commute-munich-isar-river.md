## Subject and supported account

The article concerns Benjamin David, a Munich resident whose unusual summer commute via the Isar River was reported by international media in 2017. David's own first-person account appeared in The Guardian on 29 September 2017. He described living in Munich for 34 years, managing the Kulturstrand summer arts festival near Ludwigsbrücke, and swimming approximately two kilometres downstream from his home to work.

The article reports the commute as a personal response to traffic congestion and the unpleasantness of road travel, not as a general transport recommendation. It preserves the distinction between his fastest reported time (about 12 minutes) and a more usual journey (about 27 minutes), as described in his Guardian account.

David carried work and personal belongings in a waterproof Wickelfisch bag. The bag's trapped air can provide buoyancy, but the archive does not treat it as a certified personal flotation device or a substitute for appropriate water-safety equipment.

## Safety and seasonal limits

The article makes clear that the Isar is a natural river, not a swimming pool. David described checking water conditions and avoiding unsuitable conditions. His 2017 account discussed changing currents, cold water, storm-related rises in flow, floating debris, and sharp objects on the riverbed.

The article also avoids saying that he swam to work every day throughout the year. The reported commute was principally a summer practice. His personal estimate that conditions were suitable on approximately 95 percent of the days he observed is attributed to him and is not presented as an official safety statistic.

These historical observations are not current river-condition data, and the article does not encourage readers to imitate the commute in other rivers.

## Verification and limitations

- The central story is supported by Benjamin David's first-person Guardian account and contemporaneous reporting by Reuters, The Washington Post, and BILD.
- The article's details describe reports published in 2017; they do not establish whether David continues this commute in 2026.
- Reported journey times are not a fixed timetable and vary with conditions.
- The 95 percent figure is David's personal estimate as reported in 2017, not a formal risk assessment.
- Wickelfisch is described as a waterproof bag that can provide buoyancy; it should not be represented as guaranteed life-saving equipment.
- No claim is made that river commuting is a scalable substitute for public transport.
- No BLOON GSV/BEO row is created: the article concerns a historical human-interest story and contains no verified street-level geospatial observation or coordinate.
