## Editorial summary

The article summarizes the Indonesian National Police's 23 November 2026 announcement of six suspects in an alleged treason case reportedly connected to illegal interception of President Prabowo Subianto. It records the police account of a meeting on 3 October 2024, alleged mapping and surveillance activities, electronic evidence, witness examinations, and five locations described in press coverage as suspected operational posts.

The central argument is evidentiary restraint: a suspect designation is not a conviction; police explanations are attributed allegations, not judicial findings; and residence near a reported location does not establish involvement. The article explicitly states that no public basis had been identified, as of 24 November 2026, to connect Jalan Matahari, Jalan Bintang, Jalan Bulan, or Jalan Bumi with the case.

It also explains the limits of Google Street View: imagery can document visible streets, structures, signage, and vehicles at a capture date, but cannot establish vehicle ownership, occupants, purpose, criminal activity, or relationships between people. Similarity, proximity, and ordinary neighborhood activity are not proof.
