## Article scope as published

The WordPress article was retrieved and read. It covers Gary's foundation as a U.S. Steel company town; Palace Theatre's 1925 opening and architect John Eberson; atmospheric theatre design and vaudeville; the decline of downtown Gary and the steel industry; the January 1972 closure; later uses as Star Palace Theatre and Star Academy of Performing Arts and Sciences; a 1987 redevelopment attempt; the Jackson Five marquee and the symbolic connection to Michael Jackson's *Thriller*; and a later $85 million redevelopment proposal.

The article states the 1975 and 1976 re-use attempts, but it does not include every additional historical detail in the user's later research summary. In particular, Aldrid Black's 1968 killing, the January 1972 assault, the reported approximately $13,000 sale price, the 1994 historic-district listing, the 2009 “Jackson Five Forever” signs, and the exact demolition milestones are retained below as leads requiring differentiated sourcing, not silently added as confirmed article text.

## Strongly supported historical points

- The City of Gary describes the Palace as developed in 1925 by V.U. Young and as a former center of entertainment and downtown life. It records formal closure in January 1972.
- The City of Gary says the cinema scene in Michael Jackson's 1983 *Thriller* video was filmed in Los Angeles, while describing it as a subtle homage to Gary's Palace Theatre. This is not evidence that the music-video scene was filmed inside the Gary building.
- Historic Theatre Photos records the opening date as 26 November 1925, the 2,500-seat capacity, John Eberson as architect, and Max and Sons / Maximillian Dubois as builder for Charles J. Wolf and Vern U. Young of Gary Theatre Company.
- The 1975 Star Palace Theatre and 1976 Star Academy reuses, and the unsuccessful 1987 rehabilitation attempt, are reported by specialist theatre-history and preservation sources.
- Abandoned Atlas and other secondary histories describe the 1968 killing of Aldrid Black after a showing of *Bonnie and Clyde*, and a January 1972 assault in the women's restroom as part of the theater's troubled final years. These are reported incidents; they should not be reduced to a single proven cause of closure.
- The Jackson Five marquee was installed in connection with the 2001 Miss USA event according to one detailed retrospective account; other secondary summaries date the Miss USA event to 2002. The marquee did not mean the Jackson Five performed at the Palace. A later “Jackson Five Forever” sign after Michael Jackson's death is reported in retrospective accounts.
- The $85 million redevelopment concept is a proposal involving BWI LLC, up to 250 mixed-income units, retail/entertainment space, selective demolition of the deteriorated auditorium/movie-house section, and intended preservation/adaptive reuse of the historic street wall/façade facing Broadway and Eighth Avenue.

## Current demolition status — do not overstate

A user-supplied summary claims demolition began on 24 October 2026. This exact date and claim were not independently verified.

The City of Gary's Palace Theatre redevelopment FAQ still describes selective demolition of the movie-house portion and preservation of the historic street wall as the proposed project approach, with completion anticipated in 2027. The city's 13 October 2026 multi-site demolition announcement names Genesis Convention Center, Gordon's Department Store, City Methodist Church, and St. Mary's Mercy Hospital; it does not list Palace Theatre among those named demolition sites.

An Indiana Brownfields Program project-status document dated 8 September 2026 still lists Palace Theater at 765–795 Broadway, Gary, with a project status letter dated 22 July 2025. This listing alone does not establish whether demolition had or had not begun by 24 October 2026.

A third-party Historic Theatre Photos page currently labels the Palace Theatre “Demolished,” but does not, in the retrieved result, provide a dated demolition record. That label conflicts with the City of Gary page's still-proposed language and must not be treated as proof of the exact date or scope of demolition.

**Archive conclusion:** The auditorium/movie-house section was planned for selective demolition while the historic street wall/façade was intended for preservation. The claim “demolition began 24 October 2026” remains unverified. Do not write “the entire Palace Theatre was demolished” without dated, site-specific evidence. Distinguish demolition of the auditorium from retention of the Broadway/Eighth Avenue street-wall portion.

## Other open points and discrepancies

1. **Opening date:** The retrieved article uses 26 November 1925. Some summaries give a different date or only “late November 1925.” Prefer the contemporary newspaper opening report and cite the exact source.
2. **Capacity:** The article uses approximately 2,500. Sources vary between about 2,458, 2,500, and nearly 3,000. Treat as approximate and avoid a false exact capacity.
3. **Construction cost:** The article says approximately US$1 million. This should remain attributed to historical reporting, not presented as an independently audited cost.
4. **Sale price:** The reported approximately US$13,000 sale price appears in secondary accounts; transaction record not independently inspected here.
5. **National Register:** A 1994 listing appears to refer to the Gary City Center Historic District. Do not imply Palace Theatre was necessarily individually listed without checking the nomination boundary and inventory.
6. **Crime chronology:** Aldrid Black's 1968 death and the January 1972 assault are reported in secondary accounts. Their causal relationship to closure should be phrased as contributing context, alongside financial losses and declining downtown attendance.
7. **Miss USA / marquee:** One retrospective source dates the pageant and “Jackson Five Tonite” installation to 2001; other summaries say 2002. Preserve the discrepancy until a contemporary record resolves it.
8. **Michael Jackson / Thriller:** The City of Gary's own account supports a symbolic homage, and explicitly says the scene was filmed in Los Angeles. Do not say Palace was the actual filming location.
