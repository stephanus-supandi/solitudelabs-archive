## Editorial scope

This article documents the story of Edith Macefield's house at 1438 NW 46th Street in Ballard, Seattle: its early-20th-century origins, Macefield's refusal of developers' offers in 2006, construction of Ballard Blocks around the property, her relationship with project superintendent Barry Martin, the subsequent ownership and foreclosure history, and later preservation and reuse proposals.

The article treats the house as a property-holdout case and an urban-development story, not as proof that one owner stopped an entire project or that the building was directly connected to the creation of Pixar's *Up*.

## Article summary

The house was built around 1900. Edith Wilson Macefield (1921–2008) purchased it from her mother on June 20, 1952. During planning for the Ballard Blocks commercial development, developers offered approximately US$750,000 and later US$1 million, including relocation and lifetime healthcare assistance. Macefield declined because she wanted to remain in her home.

Construction of Ballard Blocks began in spring 2006 around the property. Project superintendent Barry Martin became her friend and helped with practical daily needs. Macefield died in the house on June 15, 2008, and left it and its contents to Martin. He sold it in 2009 to Greg Pinneo of Reach Returns for approximately US$310,000. Plans to elevate the house and create Credo Square did not proceed as intended; the property entered foreclosure, and a March 2015 auction attracted no bidders.

A proposed relocation to Orcas Island by OPAL Community Land Trust did not reach its fundraising goal. Regency Centers, owner of the Ballard Blocks complex, later acquired the house in 2015 from an interim owner. In May 2024, the Seattle Daily Journal of Commerce reported that Regency and architect Chandler Stever had filed an early restoration plan and that the house was being offered to prospective commercial tenants. That report said the restoration had not yet been completed.

The article also clarifies the popular association with Pixar's *Up*. The house became known as the “Up House” and Disney/Pixar publicists used it for a balloon promotion in 2009. However, the film was already in development before Macefield's 2006 refusal, and reporting citing Pixar's denial states that her house was not the film's direct inspiration.
