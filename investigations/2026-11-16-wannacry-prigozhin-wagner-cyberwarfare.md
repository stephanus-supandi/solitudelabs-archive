## Editorial scope

The article compares three distinct elements of contemporary conflict:
1. WannaCry, a ransomware campaign publicly attributed by the UK and US governments and several allies to North Korean actors associated with the Lazarus Group.
2. Internet Research Agency (IRA), an influence-operation organization linked to Yevgeny Prigozhin through funding and management structures described in US legal proceedings.
3. NotPetya, a destructive cyber operation publicly attributed by the UK government to Russia and specifically to the Russian military intelligence service GRU.

The comparison is contextual, not a claim that these organizations coordinated or shared command.

## Core findings and evidence status

### WannaCry
- The global outbreak began on 12 May 2017.
- The ransomware used a self-propagating worm mechanism and exploited Windows vulnerability MS17-010 through the EternalBlue exploit.
- The UK government stated on 19 December 2017 that its National Cyber Security Centre assessed it was highly likely that North Korean actors known as Lazarus Group were behind the campaign.
- The US government publicly attributed the attack to North Korea on 19 December 2017; Microsoft also linked the campaign to ZINC/Lazarus.
- Public attribution is an official government assessment supported by technical investigations; it should not be represented as a judicial finding against Prigozhin or Wagner.

### Prigozhin and Internet Research Agency
- US Department of Justice proceedings described Prigozhin as funding and supporting the Internet Research Agency through companies associated with him.
- IRA's activity is characterized as online influence operations, including fabricated personas and social-media activity.
- This establishes a documented connection between Prigozhin and an influence-operation network. It does not establish that IRA created, deployed, financed, or coordinated WannaCry.

### NotPetya
- NotPetya spread globally in June 2017 after initially causing severe disruption in Ukraine.
- On 15 February 2018, the UK government publicly attributed the operation to the Russian government.
- UK statements subsequently identified the GRU as responsible.
- NotPetya and WannaCry are distinct incidents, with different technical characteristics and public attributions.

## Key analytical boundary

No credible evidence was identified in the reviewed material that Yevgeny Prigozhin, Wagner Group, or the Internet Research Agency was responsible for WannaCry or coordinated its operation with Lazarus Group.

The fact that WannaCry, IRA, Wagner, GRU, and NotPetya appear in the broader landscape of international cyber and hybrid conflict does not make them one organization or one command structure.

Do not infer operational links from:
- shared geopolitical context;
- simultaneous or nearby dates;
- the use of cyber tools or propaganda by different actors;
- alleged or documented ties to state institutions;
- the fact that multiple actors benefit from instability.

A direct link would require evidence such as technical infrastructure overlap, communications, financial transfers, shared personnel, tasking documents, or reliable investigative findings.

## Publication content summary

The article introduces WannaCry and its May 2017 global outbreak; explains the EternalBlue/MS17-010 mechanism and the public attribution to Lazarus Group; then distinguishes Prigozhin's documented relationship with the Internet Research Agency from the ransomware campaign. It introduces NotPetya as a separate 2017 cyber incident publicly attributed to Russia/GRU, and concludes by comparing digital attacks, influence operations, and armed contracting as different instruments within modern conflict.

The article's central distinctions are:
- WannaCry is not Wagner.
- IRA is not Lazarus.
- NotPetya is not WannaCry.
- A comparison of conflict instruments is not proof of a unified command.
