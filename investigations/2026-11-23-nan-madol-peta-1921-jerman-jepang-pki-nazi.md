## Summary

Menyelidiki Nan Madol di Pohnpei sebagai kompleks arkeologi pra-kolonial, lalu menempatkan penelitian Jerman (Viktor Berg pada 1907 dan ekspedisi Paul Hambruch 1908–1910), administrasi mandat Jepang setelah Perang Dunia I, serta penelitian Jepang pada 1930-an dalam kronologi yang tepat. Artikel membedakan fakta penelitian kolonial dan catatan bahwa sebagian hasil penelitian Jepang tidak dipublikasikan dari hipotesis operasi rahasia atau rekonstruksi sejarah yang belum didukung bukti. Ia juga memisahkan kronologi awal DAP/NSDAP dari ekspedisi 1910, serta perkembangan ISDV/PKI dari dugaan hubungan organisasi dengan Pohnpei. Kesimpulan: Nan Madol nyata dan jauh mendahului kolonialisme; peta 1921 menunjukkan perubahan geopolitik Pasifik, tetapi tidak membuktikan hubungan rahasia PKI–Nazi–Jepang atau pembangunan ulang situs.

## Evidence distinctions

- **Documented in the published investigation:** Nan Madol's precolonial archaeological setting; German colonial-era research; the post-WWI transfer of former German Pacific islands north of the equator to Japanese administration under the League of Nations mandate system; references to Japanese archaeological work during the 1930s.
- **Chronological correction:** Paul Hambruch's 1910 fieldwork predates the DAP (1919) and NSDAP name (1920); it should not be described as a Nazi expedition.
- **Organizational distinction:** ISDV (1914), Perserikatan Komunis di Hindia (1920), and the use of the PKI name (1924) are separate milestones. Temporal proximity does not establish organizational coordination.
- **Open archival question:** The 1992 preservation plan is cited as saying that information from Japanese-period research was not published. The identities of researchers, institutions, reports, maps, photographs, and artifact locations remain research targets.
- **Unproven hypothesis:** No evidence is presented that PKI, Nazi Germany, Japanese colonial administration, or the Indonesian state formed a secret network to construct or secretly reconstruct Nan Madol.
- **Conservation vs. reconstruction:** Modern conservation activity is not, by itself, evidence of wholesale rebuilding or falsification. Testing such a claim would require comparison of historical photographs, maps, excavation records, conservation logs, and physical evidence.

## Open research questions

1. Locate and compare Paul Hambruch's field notes, maps, photographs, and collected materials.
2. Identify Japanese researchers and institutions active at Nan Madol in the 1930s; locate any surviving reports, maps, photographs, and collections.
3. Trace the relevant League of Nations mandate documents and administrative records for Pohnpei.
4. Compare historical documentation with modern conservation records before making claims about restoration or reconstruction.
5. Investigate any proposed Indonesia/PKI connection only through concrete evidence of people, institutions, funding, correspondence, or activity—not chronology or geographic proximity.
