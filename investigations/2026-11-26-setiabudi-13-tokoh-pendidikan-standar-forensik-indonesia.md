## Scope and summary

This BLOON Investigation article asks who led the relevant Indonesian police, criminology, forensic, prosecution, and judicial institutions around the time of Setiabudi 13; where selected figures studied; and how international knowledge and forensic standards entered Indonesian institutions.

The article covers the institutional and educational context from 1952 to 1985, with brief references to later developments. Its central methodological warning is that an individual's overseas education, a foreign institutional partnership, the formal adoption of a procedure, and the actual use of that procedure in a specific investigation are four different claims. One does not prove the next.

The article is a contextual reconstruction. It does not claim that every named person personally investigated Setiabudi 13 or handled its evidence.

## People and institutional context

### Police leadership

**Jenderal Polisi Awaloedin Djamin**
- Kapolri from 26 September 1978 to 3 December 1982; therefore he was Kapolri when Setiabudi 13 occurred.
- Studied at PTIK in Jakarta (1955), then completed a Master of Public Administration at the University of Pittsburgh and a doctorate at the University of Southern California (1963).
- His US education is relevant to police administration and institutional history. It is not evidence that FBI procedures were applied in the Setiabudi 13 investigation.

**Jenderal Polisi Anton Soedjarwo**
- Kapolri from 4 December 1982 to 6 June 1986; he was not Kapolri on the incident date.
- The article records Officer Orientation School at Fort Benning, United States (1961).
- The Fort Benning course is military training, not proof of forensic laboratory specialization.

### Criminology and forensic medicine

**Prof. Mardjono Reksodiputro**
- Law degree from Universitas Indonesia (1961).
- MA in Criminology from the University of Pennsylvania (1967).
- Relevant to the development of Indonesian criminological thought, criminal law, and the criminal justice system.
- Criminology is not interchangeable with forensic laboratory examination or crime-scene processing.

**dr. Abdul Mun’im Idries**
- Medical degree from FKUI (1971).
- Forensic medicine specialist qualification from FKUI (1979).
- The article records an aviation-health pathology course at Lakespra Saryanto (1975) and a dactyloscopy course at the Polri Criminal Laboratory (1980).
- It also records roles at the University of Indonesia's criminology and forensic institutions, including as secretary of the academic division (1979–1983) and as head of an LKUI–IKF FKUI–PMJ crime-scene examination team from 1979.
- His professional background makes him relevant to forensic practice in that period. However, the article explicitly says no primary document has yet established that he personally examined the Setiabudi 13 scene or remains.

### Criminal laboratory and international cooperation

The article traces the Polri Criminal Laboratory to 15 January 1954, when Interpol and laboratory sections were established within the criminal investigation service. Early work included document examination, firearms examination, and ballistics.

Indonesia began participating in ICPO/Interpol activities in 1952 and became a member in 1954. The article also notes a secondary historical study describing laboratory-training assistance in the context of the Marshall Plan. It treats that reference as a lead for further archival research, not as a complete account of transferred curricula, named trainees, or specific forensic methods.

**Ali Said**
- Attorney General, 1973–1981.

**Ismail Saleh**
- Attorney General, 1981–1984.
- The article lists domestic legal and military education, including the Akademi Hukum Militer, Perguruan Tinggi Hukum Militer, an Army general-administration course, and Seskoad.

**Prof. Oemar Seno Adji**
- Chief Justice of the Supreme Court, 1974–1981.
- Legal education at Rechtshogeschool and the Faculty of Law, Universitas Gadjah Mada (1949).

**Mudjono, S.H.**
- Chief Justice of the Supreme Court, 1981–1984.
- The article records education at the Akademi Hukum Militer.
- The reviewed material did not establish formal overseas education for either Oemar Seno Adji or Mudjono.
- Holding the office of Chief Justice does not mean either person automatically supervised or handled the Setiabudi 13 investigation.

### Later generation: Roy Suryo

Roy Suryo was born on 18 July 1968 and was about 13 when Setiabudi 13 occurred. The article records his later education in communications at Universitas Gadjah Mada and a master's program in health behavior and promotion. His public career as a legislator and Minister of Youth and Sports came decades after the case.

The article places him in the later history of Indonesian information technology and telematics discourse, not among the forensic or criminological professionals of 1981. The education described does not establish formal training in criminology, forensic medicine, or criminal laboratory work.

## Four distinctions that govern the research

1. **Individual education transfer:** a person studied abroad or attended a foreign course.
2. **Institutional transfer:** an Indonesian institution received foreign assistance, training, equipment, or technical cooperation.
3. **Formal technical adoption:** a method became part of an Indonesian rule, manual, curriculum, or operating procedure.
4. **Case-level application:** investigators actually used that method in the Setiabudi 13 investigation.

Evidence for one level cannot be used as automatic proof of the next. In particular, a foreign degree held by a police chief does not establish that a particular foreign agency's crime-scene protocol was used in a particular case.

## What remains unverified

- Which doctors, police officers, and laboratory personnel actually examined the Setiabudi 13 scene and remains.
- Whether the original autopsy report, forensic photographs, laboratory notes, fingerprint records, and evidence inventory survive.
- Which crime-scene security and evidence-handling procedures were in force and used on 23 November 1981.
- Whether every recovered item and body fragment was photographed, numbered, packaged, sealed, and logged.
- Whether a contemporaneous chain-of-custody record exists.
- Which foreign-assisted courses took place before 1981, who attended them, and what their curricula contained.
- Whether any specific US, European, or Australian protocol was formally adopted and then applied in this investigation.
- The precise handover dates for the Attorney General and Chief Justice offices during calendar year 1981.
