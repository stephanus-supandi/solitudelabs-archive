## Archive summary

The article examines Leak through Balinese folklore and belief, lontar-related cultural discussions, classical and modern readings of the Calon Arang narrative, traditional performance, and the modern horror film *Mystics in Bali* (1981).

The central methodological distinction is between:
1. A cultural phenomenon that can be studied through texts, performance, oral tradition, social practice, and popular representation.
2. Supernatural claims, which should not be presented as empirically established facts without independently verifiable evidence.

## Main topics

### Leak and pengleakan

The article challenges the narrow popular image of Leak as merely a frightening flying head from horror cinema. It discusses Leak and pengleakan as concepts represented in Balinese belief and cultural materials. Terminology and meanings should not be reduced to one universal definition; they may vary by source, region, tradition, and context.

### Pangiwa and Panengen

The article refers to cultural documentation discussing Pangiwa and Panengen. These concepts should not be flattened into a universal, simplistic equation of “evil” and “good.” Any stronger interpretation requires context-specific sources.

### Calon Arang and Rangda

Calon Arang is discussed as a figure in a literary and folkloric narrative involving Empu Bharadah. Modern readings may interpret the story through gender, stigma, social exclusion, and power, but these are interpretive approaches rather than the only possible meaning.

Rangda appears in performance contexts and should not be treated as automatically identical to Leak. Their relationship must be described in the context of the particular text, performance, or ritual being discussed.

### Cinema and reverse involution

The article refers to research by M. V. Frolova (2026) on sorcery in Balinese tradition and contemporary culture, including the horror film *Mystics in Bali*. The term “reverse involution” is used in that research to discuss how cinematic representations can feed back into popular imagination and folklore.

This does **not** establish that every popular image of Leak originated in cinema, nor that film representations are a complete record of older traditions.

## BLOON audit notes and corrections to avoid overclaiming

- “Leak is an ilmu, not a ghost” is too absolute as a universal definition. Use context-sensitive wording.
- The analogy that pengleakan is a neutral tool like a printing machine is illustrative, not established ethnographic fact.
- Do not state that Barong and Rangda are simply twins or that Balinese cosmology universally means good and evil must remain in balance unless supported by specific sources.
- “Leak has no physical evidence” is imprecise. The defensible statement is that supernatural transformation claims have not been established by verified empirical evidence.
- Do not identify living people as Leak or repeat accusations against named individuals without reliable evidence and careful legal/ethical review.
- Distinguish a modern literary interpretation of Calon Arang as a stigmatized woman from a claim that this is the definitive original meaning.
- Do not infer that all contemporary visualizations of Leak derive from *Mystics in Bali*.
