# Nord Stream, Bank Artha Graha Internasional, dan Tomy Winata — Research Dossier

**Prepared:** 2026-11-11  
**Type:** Working research dossier  
**Status:** Not a published article; preliminary source audit  
**Scope:** Examine whether documented Nord Stream financing institutions can be connected, through evidence, to PT Bank Artha Graha Internasional Tbk (BAGI) and Tomy Winata.

## Executive finding

Three separate propositions must not be collapsed:

1. **Tomy Winata and BAGI:** BAGI's 2024 Annual Report identifies Tomy Winata and Sugianto Kusuma as the bank's ultimate controlling shareholders through five named shareholder companies. The same report records Tomy Winata as Vice President Commissioner. BAGI's own June 2026 AGM announcement again lists him as Vice President Commissioner.
2. **Nord Stream and international banks:** Nord Stream 1 reached a €3.9 billion Phase I financing close in March 2010, with a syndicated commercial-bank group of 26 banks. A specialist legal adviser to lenders and export-credit agencies confirms the 26-bank syndicate. Trade-finance reporting identifies selected lenders, including Commerzbank, Deutsche Bank, ING, Société Générale, UniCredit, BNP Paribas, Crédit Agricole, and others.
3. **BAGI and Nord Stream:** The sources reviewed for this dossier do **not** establish that BAGI, Tomy Winata, or an Artha Graha Network entity financed Nord Stream, arranged its financing, guaranteed it, or participated in a transaction connected to the pipeline. No direct or indirect transaction link is established here.

The first two propositions are documented independently. They do not prove the third.

## 1. Tomy Winata and Bank Artha Graha Internasional

### 1.1 Governance position

BAGI's 2024 Annual Report records Tomy Winata as **Wakil Komisaris Utama / Vice President Commissioner**. It states that he first held the role in 2005 and records his reappointment by a shareholders' meeting resolution dated 17 July 2023.

The bank's AGM announcement dated 17 June 2026 again lists Tomy Winata as Vice President Commissioner. This is a later company disclosure and should be used for the reported 2026 board composition.

### 1.2 Ultimate controlling shareholders

The 2024 Annual Report's section on major and controlling shareholders states that Tomy Winata and Sugianto Kusuma are the shareholders of five intermediary shareholder companies:

- PT Pirus Platinum Murni
- PT Cerana Arthaputra
- PT Puspita Bisnispuri
- PT Arthamulia Sentosajaya
- PT Karya Nusantara Permai

The report consequently identifies Tomy Winata and Sugianto Kusuma as BAGI's **ultimate controlling shareholders**.

Important distinction: the report's separate table of direct share ownership by commissioners records no direct shareholding for Tomy Winata. This does not contradict the ultimate-control disclosure; it distinguishes direct personal share ownership from control exercised through shareholder entities. The two descriptions must not be conflated.

## 2. Nord Stream 1: documented bank financing

Nord Stream's Phase I financing closed on 16 March 2010 at **€3.9 billion**. Lenz & Staehelin, a law firm advising export-credit agencies and lenders on the financing, describes the debt package as involving a commercial bank syndicate of **26 banks**, alongside export-credit support from Hermes, SACE, and Germany's UFK programme.

Global Trade Review's deal record identifies a number of participating and arranging banks. Selected names include:

- Commerzbank
- Deutsche Bank
- ING
- Société Générale
- UniCredit
- BNP Paribas
- Crédit Agricole CIB
- RBS / ABN AMRO
- DZ Bank
- Natixis
- SMBC

This list is a selected roster from trade-finance reporting, not a claim that every named institution had the same role. Lead arrangers, participants, agents, advisers, account banks, and export-credit agencies are different roles. The dossier does not flatten them into one category.

Lenz & Staehelin's deal note independently supports the financing amount and 26-bank syndicate, but does not itself publish the full lender roster.

## 3. Nord Stream 2: distinguish corporate financing from bank lending

On 24 April 2017, Nord Stream 2 AG announced financing agreements with five European energy companies: ENGIE, OMV, Shell, Uniper, and Wintershall. Together they committed long-term financing for 50% of the project's then-estimated €9.5 billion cost, up to €950 million each. Gazprom remained the sole shareholder of Nord Stream 2 AG.

These commitments were made by **energy companies**, not a list of five commercial banks. They must not be described as a bank syndicate. The announcement does not establish that BAGI or Tomy Winata participated.

## 4. What BAGI's disclosures establish about international banking

BAGI's 2024 Annual Report describes responsibilities that include maintaining relations with correspondent banks, investment banks, financial institutions, customers, and monetary authorities in Indonesia and abroad.

The same report's financial statements describe current accounts and placements with other banks as balance-sheet categories. Those generic categories do not, by themselves, identify a Nord Stream lender or prove a Nord Stream-related transaction.

For this dossier, the existence of international banking relationships is treated as a legitimate research lead, not as proof of a specific relationship with any Nord Stream lender. The annual-report text reviewed here does not identify a Nord Stream transaction, nor does it provide evidence that BAGI financed the pipeline.

## 6. Next verification tasks before an article

1. Obtain BAGI annual reports and audited financial statements for the relevant years around 2009–2011 and 2017, not only the 2024 report.
2. Identify named correspondent banks from primary BAGI disclosures, if disclosed, and preserve the reporting period and legal entity.
3. Compare those names with the full Nord Stream 1 lender roster and distinguish lender, arranger, agent, adviser, and export-credit agency roles.
4. Check whether any match is a parent group, subsidiary, branch, or separately incorporated Indonesian entity. Similar names do not establish the same legal person.
5. Search BAGI, Artha Graha Network, Tomy Winata, and relevant subsidiaries in project-finance announcements, audited related-party disclosures, court/arbitration records, and official sanctions documents.
6. For any claimed payment path, require transaction-level evidence: counterparties, dates, legal entities, currency, payment purpose, and source documentation. Do not infer a transaction from a correspondent-bank relationship alone.
7. Treat absence from the documents reviewed as **not located**, not proof that no relationship ever existed.

## 7. Method and limitations

- Corporate office, ultimate control, correspondent banking, project finance, advisory work, and payment processing are distinct relationship types.
- A bank's participation in one syndicated loan does not imply that every branch, subsidiary, or representative office of the banking group participated.
- A correspondent relationship does not prove that a specific transaction passed through that relationship.
- Shared nationality, overlapping bank names, geographic proximity, or common business networks do not establish ownership, coordination, or a transaction.
- This is a preliminary source audit, not a forensic examination of private bank records.
- No allegation of misconduct is made.
- No claim is made that BAGI or Tomy Winata had a Nord Stream role.
