# Gang Life in the Neighborhood — Working Field Note

**Prepared:** 2026-11-06  
**Type:** Working field note / conversational observation  
**Status:** Unverified personal report; not a formal neighborhood survey

## 1. Scope

This note preserves a small set of observations and remarks raised during a conversation about everyday life in a residential alley. The humorous working frame is **“Fauna dan Kehidupan Sosial Komplek”**—a satire of everyday roles and behavior, not a classification of residents.

## 2. Observations and reported remarks

The conversation mentioned:

- Cars entering and leaving the alley.
- Motorcycles entering and leaving the alley.
- A person sweeping the neighborhood in an unhurried manner, described jokingly as **“Penyapu Santuy.”**
- The neighborhood head (Pak RT), mentioned as part of the local social environment.
- The user reported that the previous day the neighborhood head had asked for a residents' contribution (iuran).

No date-specific field log, photograph, vehicle count, exact location, contribution amount, stated purpose, collection period, receipt, budget, or expenditure report was supplied in the conversation.

## 3. What is not established

This note does **not** establish:

- That any driver behaved dangerously or violated traffic rules.
- That the person sweeping is formally employed or assigned to a particular task.
- That the requested contribution was improper, compulsory, excessive, or misused.
- The amount, purpose, frequency, authorization, or accounting of the contribution.
- Any misconduct by the neighborhood head or any resident.

The phrase **“Mobil Goblok”** or **“Motor Goblok”** is a satirical label for potentially inconsiderate behavior, not a factual finding about the people or vehicles mentioned.

## 4. Questions for a future, evidence-based neighborhood note

If the topic is revisited, record only details that can be checked:

1. What is the stated purpose of the contribution?
2. What amount and collection period were communicated?
3. Was the request made under an agreed neighborhood decision or another documented basis?
4. Is there a receipt, cashbook, budget, or periodic report available to residents?
5. What specific traffic behavior was observed, at what place and time, and what safety impact did it have?

Questions are not allegations. A request for transparent information is not proof of wrongdoing.

## 5. Editorial framing

The comic concept can describe the shared ecosystem of a neighborhood alley: vehicles, cleaning work, residents, and local administration. Humor should target observable behavior and unclear systems—not invent motives, accuse individuals, or turn an incomplete anecdote into a verdict.

A useful distinction:

- **Observation:** something directly seen or recorded.
- **Reported:** something someone says happened.
- **Inference:** an interpretation drawn from available information.
- **Unknown:** information not yet established.
